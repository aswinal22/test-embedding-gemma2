# Sourdough Proofing

The part of bread making people most often rush, and the part that decides
whether the loaf is good.

## Bulk fermentation

After mixing, the dough rests at room temperature until it roughly doubles in
size. At 24C this takes four to six hours; at 18C it can take eight or more.

Watch for three signals rather than the clock:

- The dough has risen by about fifty percent, not merely doubled.
- The surface is domed rather than flat.
- There are visible bubbles at the edges of the dough.

A container that has more than doubled in height is a better guide than an
alarm.

## Shaping and the second rise

Shape, then refrigerate for an overnight cold proof. The cold rise develops
flavour and makes scoring easier, because cold dough is far less sticky.

Bake straight from the refrigerator into a preheated Dutch oven. The steam
trapped in the pot gives the crust its initial spread.

## Common problems

Dense crumb usually means under-fermented dough, or a starter that was not
active enough. An oven spring that tears the crust unevenly means the dough was
over-proofed and had lost its structure.
