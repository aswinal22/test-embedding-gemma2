# Major Chords

A major chord is three notes stacked in thirds, and it is the most consonant
sound in Western music.

## The intervals

From the root, the major third is four semitones and the perfect fifth is
seven. A C major chord is C, E, G.

- C to E: four semitones, major third
- C to G: seven semitones, perfect fifth
- E to G: three semitones, minor third

That last one is why major chords feel stable. The minor third on top pulls
slightly, and that tension is what makes the chord want to resolve.

## Inversions

The same three notes can be rearranged without changing the chord:

- Root position: C E G
- First inversion: E G C
- Second inversion: G C E

The lowest note changes, and so does the tension it creates. Bass notes and
inversions are one of the main tools composers use to make a progression move.

## Building chords from scales

In any major or minor scale, take every other note starting from the root and
stack thirds. In C major that gives C major, D minor, E minor, F major, G major,
A minor, B diminished. Each degree of the scale has a chord quality attached to
it, and the pattern of major, minor and diminished is what tonal music is made
of.
