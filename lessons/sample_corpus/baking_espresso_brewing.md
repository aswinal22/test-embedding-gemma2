# Espresso Brewing

Espresso is defined by pressure and a specific ratio, not by any one recipe.

## The parameters

- 18g of coffee, ground fine
- 36g of espresso in the cup, a one to two ratio
- Nine bars of pressure
- Water at 93C
- Around 28 seconds from first drop

## What each parameter does

**Grind** controls extraction. Too coarse and water races through, leaving the
coffee underextracted and sour. Too fine and it chokes, overextracting and
tasting bitter.

**Dose and ratio** control strength. Using more coffee for the same yield gives
a stronger shot; using less gives a weaker one.

**Pressure** forces water through the puck. Below about six bars, and below
what is called a true espresso, which is defined around nine.

**Temperature** controls which compounds dissolve. Too cool and the sour
compounds extract; too hot and the bitter ones do.

**Time** is the summary of all of the above. If everything else is right, the
time follows.

## Dialing in

Change one variable at a time. If it is sour, grind finer or brew hotter. If it
is bitter, grind coarser or brew cooler. Adjust the grinder by small steps and
taste again — chasing several variables at once is how beginners get stuck.
