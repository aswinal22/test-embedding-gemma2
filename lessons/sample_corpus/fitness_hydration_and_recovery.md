# Hydration and Recovery

The boring fundamentals that decide whether a training programme works.

## Drinking water consistently

Sipping steadily through the day beats drinking large amounts at once. The
gut absorbs fluid best at small regular intervals, and by the time you feel
genuinely thirsty you are already behind.

For most people, roughly 30 to 40 millilitres per kilogram of body weight per
day is a reasonable baseline, more in heat or during long sessions.

Colour is a usable guide. Pale straw means fine. Dark amber means you have
been behind for hours.

## What counts as hydration

Water, and tea and coffee to a lesser degree. Alcohol and heavily sweetened
drinks work against you: they add to the total the body must process while
contributing little of what you need.

## Sodium

Lost in sweat, and it is the component most often replaced improperly. During
sessions longer than about an hour in heat, or any session with heavy sweating,
sodium matters more than it does in ordinary conditions.

## Sleep

Seven to nine hours is where adaptation happens. Training provides the stimulus;
sleep is when the tissue actually rebuilds. A programme that increases load
while cutting sleep is asking for injury, and the numbers on the watch will look
fine right up until they do not.
