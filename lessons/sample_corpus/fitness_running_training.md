# Running Training

Injury in distance running is usually load management, not bad running form.

## The ten percent rule

Increase total weekly distance by no more than about ten percent per week.
Anything more is the most common route to a stress injury.

Volume matters more than intensity. A runner adding five kilometres a week
while continuing to run intervals three times a week is increasing load far
faster than the rule suggests, because the intervals were never counted in the
first place.

## Weeks off are training

A cutback week every fourth week, or every two during a build, is what allows
the adaptation to happen. Tissue adapts more slowly than fitness, and most
injuries are tissue outpacing fitness.

## Strength work

Two sessions a week of basic single-leg and core strength reduces injury risk
measurably. The adaptation is not dramatic, but it is consistent, and it is far
cheaper than the alternative.

## Signs to back off

Tenderness that persists for three days. Pain that changes your gait. Soreness
that has not shifted after 48 hours. None of these mean stop running
altogether, but all of them mean do not add distance this week.
