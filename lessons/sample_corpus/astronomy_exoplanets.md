# Exoplanet Detection

Planets orbiting stars other than our Sun are extraordinarily common, and almost
all of them are found by indirect methods. The planet itself is almost never
seen.

## The transit method

A planet crossing in front of its star blocks a small fraction of the star's
light. The brightness dips, periodically, by a predictable amount.

Two things fall out of that dip. The **depth** gives the planet's size relative
to the star. The **period** between repeats gives its orbital period, and with
that its distance.

## The radial velocity method

A planet tugging on its star makes the star orbit a point slightly off-centre.
That wobble shifts the star's spectral lines periodically — the Doppler effect.
The size of the shift constrains the planet's mass.

## Why indirect

The angle between a planet's orbit and our line of sight is almost always very
small, so the planet is hidden behind or lost in the glare of its star. Direct
imaging is possible only for young, widely separated planets.

## Limits

Both methods have selection effects. Transits require a favourable alignment and
miss most planets. Radial velocity favours large planets close to their stars.
Together they still miss most of what is out there.
